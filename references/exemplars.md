# Exemplar case studies (studied 2026-10-15)

What each case study does well, what to borrow, and what to avoid. Add new exemplars at the bottom in the same format.

---

## James Chugh: The Coupon Hunter (EazyDiner)
https://www.jameschugh.com/work/eazydiner · homepage https://www.jameschugh.com/#work

- **Homepage work section:** the section title is a claim with a highlighter ("Things I shipped, and the numbers they moved."). Cards rise out of a 3D box on scroll, each with a logo, a nickname, and **one hero metric** (+41%, 2×). A plain numbered index below is the readable fallback. Clicking lifts the card out, then opens the case study.
- **Nickname + company:** "The Coupon Hunter · EazyDiner · PayEazy". Memorable and credible.
- **Case study structure:** comic-strip story opener (dinner → bill → too many offers → best deal). A **flippable summary card** styled as a restaurant bill (role, timeline, team, before → after metrics). Chapters with story titles ("Chapter 1 · The drop-off", "Chapter 7 · The twist").
- **Interactive proofs:** follow 100 diners through the funnel; type 8 card digits to see only the offers that apply; a before/after toggle.
- **Honest labels:** "(projected)", "Targets, launching soon".
- **Handoff as a beat:** a 3-hour dev deep-dive, plus agreed success metrics.
- **Borrow:** nicknames, one interactive proof, the summary card, the handoff beat, story chapter titles.
- **Avoid:** the 3D box hurts readability at rest; projects repeated 3×; very long (2,500+ words).

## Rahul R Nadkarni: Wonders (Headout)
https://rahulrn.framer.website/wonders

- **Header:** company chip + scale ("49 million+ guests served") · outcome-led title ("…to increase mid-week retention") · a **GOAL metric** (5% of DAU by week 4), because the feature is on hold · a Team block (1 Designer 👋, UX Eng, PM, UX Writer · 5 weeks).
- **Sticky section rail:** Problem → Solution direction → Constraints → Iterations → Implementation → Edge case → Development.
- **"Thinking" callouts:** mono-labelled asides that show his reasoning at each decision.
- **Every option gets a verdict:** an iteration board + caption + ✓ pros / – cons; placement options closed with "Shared discovery tabs won".
- **Constraints section:** "What Wonders was not allowed to be".
- **Edge case section:** "What if I start 20 weeks into the launch?"
- **Honest status:** "put on hold". A sticky **TL;DR** button. Sign-off: "Made it this far? Talk to me alreadyy".
- **Borrow:** the GOAL-instead-of-result pattern, team block, rail, thinking callouts, verdicts, edge case, TL;DR.
- **Avoid:** huge boards shrunk unreadable; plain visuals; around 2,000 words.

## Eemon Roy: Researcher Agent on the Taskbar (Microsoft)
https://www.eemonroy.com/researcher · Wall of Portfolios #1, Sep 2026

- **The cleanest And/But/Therefore loop.** Hook "The day Copilot stopped answering questions and started doing work" → **But** "once users left the app, the agent effectively disappeared (This was a problem)" → **Reframe** "Show progress on the taskbar. NO. How do you help users trust work they cannot see?" → **Therefore** dozens of states reduced to 4 → **But** "What happens when one agent becomes many?" → **Therefore** a single presence → **But** "An edge case that changed the design" → **Therefore** a hand-raise state → Outcome (Ignite, 2M WAU) → Reflection on AI design.
- **Meta:** Duration · Role · Users (2M WAU) · Status (LIVE).
- **Human analogy** for the expectation: "Imagine asking a colleague to prepare a report…"
- Around 900 words.
- **Borrow:** the reframe beat, edge-case-as-plot, the closing reflection, the analogy.

## Dharmesh Nayak: Meals under your budget (Zomato)
https://dharmeshnayak.com/projects/meals-under-your-budget · WoP #2

- **The hook is a vivid behaviour:** "cart-hopping", where users built carts at several restaurants just to see the final price.
- Hypothesis → idea → ambitious first direction → **But** the build was too long → **Therefore** "Scoping down to ship".
- **Honest impact:** order-through rate up, AOV dipped ("the expected trade"), with segment detail.
- **Withholds depth on purpose:** "The full story has more… get in touch for a walkthrough."
- Around 600 words. Meta: My role (a skills list) · Team · Timeline.
- **Borrow:** the human-behaviour hook, the scope-down beat, honest trade-offs, the walkthrough CTA.

## Aditya Muralidhar: Category discoverability (FirstClub)
https://adityamuralidhar.in/work/firstclub-discoverability · WoP #3

- **3 numbers before the story** (57.7% conversion, 40+ categories, 4.4× impressions).
- A one-line hook-insight: "Curation without visibility is just inventory no one sees."
- **But** "the pattern itself isn't new" → **Therefore** the twist is a media-rich first card.
- **But** the second widget converted at 0.43% → **Reframe** it as doing an awareness job.
- "What I would do differently", plus the team thanked by name.
- **Borrow:** proof first, the one-line insight, reframing a weak metric honestly, credit.

## Vaibhav Verma: Video-first car detail page (Cars24)
https://vaibhaverma.netlify.app/cars24 · WoP #4

- **A TL;DR at the very top:** Problem / Solution / Impact, plus a link to the Figma working file.
- Numbered sections with story headings ("A trust gap you can't close from a photo.").
- **But** "18/20 found the video too long" → **Therefore** a jump-to-section rail.
- **"The pilot, honestly":** why the video concept was shelved, and what survived.
- **Every paragraph has a long and a short (mobile) version.**
- Impact: ~18% funnel lift, held over time; usability findings with counts.
- Around 1,800 words.
- **Borrow:** the top TL;DR, the "honestly" section, test findings driving design, dual-length copy.

## Vaibhav Shukla: How we saved 300 crib returns (Cradlewise)
https://vaibhavshukla.xyz/Dashboard-revamp

- **The title is the business outcome.** Meta: Role · Collaborators (named) · Timeline · Outcome.
- **"Why was this project prioritized?"**: support queries, 90% solvable with a clearer app.
- **Headings tell the story:** "DAY 1: I started with a skeleton" (old TV-set metaphor) · "We were stuck, our customers came to rescue" · "IDEA #Nth 🎉" · "FINALLY WE REACHED TO".
- **"Why failed?" after every idea:** honest bullets, then the next idea.
- **Sticky-note annotations** beside mockups.
- **Closest analogue to Suraj's Eventory vendor app** (a daily-use operational app, prioritized by support load).
- **Borrow:** the outcome title, the why-prioritized beat, "Why failed?", stickies, story headings.
- **Avoid:** the outcome never proven at the end; no reflection; everything as a heading; typos; blank gaps.

---

## Cross-cutting patterns
1. The hook is a human moment, not a feature.
2. A reframe beat (obvious fix → real question).
3. Real "buts": edge cases, constraints, test results, failed ideas.
4. Honesty as a selling point.
5. Proof or status up top; TL;DR.
6. A one-line reflection to close.
7. Length follows depth (600–1,800 words across the exemplars).
